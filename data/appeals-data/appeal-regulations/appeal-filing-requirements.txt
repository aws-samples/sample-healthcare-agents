# Appeal Filing Regulations and Payer-Specific Requirements
# Reference for Appeals Agent — Compliance and Timelines

## Federal Regulations

### ERISA (Employee Retirement Income Security Act) — Group Health Plans
- Internal Appeal: Must be filed within 180 days of denial notice
- Payer Decision Deadline: 30 days for pre-service; 60 days for post-service
- Urgent Care: 72 hours for expedited review
- External Review: Available after exhausting internal appeals
- External Review Filing: Within 4 months of final internal denial
- External Review Decision: 45 days (standard); 72 hours (expedited)
- Required Notice: Payer must provide written denial with specific reason, plan provision, and appeal rights

### ACA (Affordable Care Act) — Additional Protections
- Minimum two levels of internal appeal required
- External review must use accredited Independent Review Organization (IRO)
- Payer must cover cost of external review
- Denial notices must be provided in culturally and linguistically appropriate manner
- Applies to non-grandfathered individual and group plans

### Medicare Appeals (5 Levels)
- Level 1 — Redetermination by MAC: File within 120 days of initial determination
  - Decision within 60 days
- Level 2 — Reconsideration by QIC: File within 180 days of redetermination
  - Decision within 60 days
- Level 3 — ALJ/OMHA Hearing: File within 60 days of reconsideration
  - Amount in controversy must meet threshold ($180 for 2025)
- Level 4 — Medicare Appeals Council Review: File within 60 days of ALJ decision
- Level 5 — Federal District Court: File within 60 days of Council decision
  - Amount in controversy must meet threshold ($1,840 for 2025)

### Medicaid Appeals
- Varies by state; generally must file within 30-90 days of denial
- Fair hearing rights guaranteed under federal law
- Continuation of benefits available if appeal filed within 10 days of denial notice
- State must render decision within 90 days (3 days for expedited)

## Payer-Specific Appeal Requirements

### Blue Cross Blue Shield
- First Level Appeal: Within 180 days of denial
  - Submit to address on Explanation of Benefits (EOB)
  - Include member ID, claim number, date of service
  - Decision within 30 days (pre-service) or 60 days (post-service)
- Second Level Appeal: Within 60 days of first level denial
  - May request independent medical review
  - Decision within 30 days
- Expedited Appeal: Available for urgent/emergent situations
  - Decision within 72 hours
- Required Documentation: Clinical notes, letter of medical necessity, peer-reviewed literature

### UnitedHealthcare
- First Level Appeal: Within 180 days of denial
  - Submit via fax, mail, or provider portal
  - Include authorization number (if applicable), clinical documentation
  - Decision within 30 days (pre-service) or 60 days (post-service)
- Second Level Appeal: Within 60 days of first level denial
  - Reviewed by physician not involved in original decision
  - Decision within 30 days
- Peer-to-Peer Review: Available before formal appeal
  - Must be requested within 10 business days of denial
  - Conducted between treating physician and UHC medical director
- Required Documentation: Updated clinical notes, treatment plan, supporting literature

### Aetna
- First Level Appeal: Within 180 days of denial
  - Submit via Availity portal, fax, or mail
  - Decision within 30 days (pre-service) or 60 days (post-service)
- Second Level Appeal: Within 60 days of first level denial
  - Independent physician review
  - Decision within 30 days
- Expedited Appeal: 72-hour turnaround for urgent cases
- Peer-to-Peer: Available within 10 business days of denial
- Required Documentation: Detailed clinical notes, diagnostic results, treatment rationale

### Cigna
- First Level Appeal: Within 180 days of denial
  - Submit via provider portal or mail
  - Decision within 30 days (pre-service) or 60 days (post-service)
- Second Level Appeal: Within 60 days of first level denial
  - Decision within 30 days
- External Review: Available after exhausting internal appeals
  - Must file within 4 months of final internal denial
- Required Documentation: Clinical records, physician letter, supporting evidence

## Appeal Letter Requirements — Common Elements

### Required Components (All Payers)
1. Patient demographics (name, DOB, member ID)
2. Claim reference (claim number, date of service, authorization number)
3. Provider information (name, NPI, contact)
4. Denial reference (denial date, denial reason code, denial letter date)
5. Specific appeal request (what action is being requested)
6. Clinical rationale (why the service is medically necessary)
7. Supporting documentation list
8. Signature of treating physician or authorized representative

### Best Practices
- Reference specific plan language and coverage criteria
- Cite peer-reviewed literature and clinical guidelines
- Include chronological clinical narrative
- Address each denial reason individually
- Request specific remedy (approve service, reprocess claim, etc.)
- Send via certified mail or trackable electronic submission
- Retain copies of all correspondence
